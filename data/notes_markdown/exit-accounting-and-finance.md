 Quality Control: The process by which a company ensures its products or services meet specified standards and customer expectations. 
 Revenue: The total income generated from the sale of goods or services before any expenses are deducted.

 Receivables: Amounts owed to a company by customers or clients for goods or services provided on credit.

 Reserves: Funds set aside by a company to cover future liabilities or uncertainties, such as bad debts or legal settlements.

 Revenue Recognition: The accounting principle that dictates when revenue should be recognized, usually when it is earned and realizable.

 Subledger: A detailed, subsidiary ledger that supports or provides detailed information for a general ledger account. 
 Sales Revenue: The income earned from the sale of goods or services, which can be used to determine profitability. 
 Statement of Cash Flows: A financial statement that shows the cash inflows and outflows of a company during a specific period, helping to assess its liquidity. 
 Salary Expense: The costs associated with paying employee salaries, a significant portion of operating expenses. 
 Stock: A share in the ownership of a company, which represents a claim on part of the company’s assets and earnings. 
 Standard Costing: The practice of assigning predetermined or "standard" costs to products, which are compared to actual costs to assess performance.

 Treasury Stock: Shares of a company’s own stock that have been repurchased and are held in its treasury, not outstanding in the market.

 Treasury Management: The process of managing a company’s cash, investments, and financial risks to optimize liquidity and minimize financial costs.

 Tangible Net Worth: A measure of a company's net worth that excludes intangible assets, like goodwill or intellectual property, to focus on tangible assets.

 Utility Expense: The costs incurred by a company for services like electricity, water, and internet, typically considered an operating expense.

 Underwriting: The process by which a financial institution evaluates and assumes the risk of a financial transaction, such as issuing insurance or bonds.
