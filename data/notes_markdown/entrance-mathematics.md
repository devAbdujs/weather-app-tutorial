Statistics: The study of the collection, analysis, interpretation, presentation, and organization of data
        Descriptive Statistics: Methods of organizing, summarizing, and presenting data in an informative way
        Inferential Statistics: Methods used to draw conclusions and make predictions from data
        Probability: The branch of mathematics concerned with probability theory, the analysis of random phenomena
        Probability Distribution: A function that describes the likelihood of obtaining the possible values that a random variable can take
        Normal Distribution: A probability distribution that is symmetric about the mean, showing that data near the mean are more frequent in occurrence than data far from the mean
        Central Limit Theorem: A fundamental theorem in probability theory that states that the distribution of sample means approaches a normal distribution as the sample size gets larger
        Hypothesis Testing: A statistical method that uses sample data to evaluate a hypothesis about a population parameter
        Regression Analysis: A statistical technique used to determine the relationship between variables
        Correlation: A statistical measure that indicates the extent to which two or more variables fluctuate together
    Linear Regression: A linear approach to modeling the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables
        Chi-Square Test: A statistical test used to determine whether there is a significant association between the categorical variables
        T-Test: A statistical test used to determine if there is a significant difference between the means of two groups
        Analysis of Variance (ANOVA): A statistical method used to test differences between two or more means
        Time Series Analysis: A statistical technique that deals with time series data, aiming to extract meaningful statistics and other characteristics of the data
        Sampling Techniques: Methods used to select a subset of individuals from a population to estimate characteristics of the whole population
        Qualitative Data Analysis: Analysis of non-numerical data such as text, images, or videos
        Quantitative Data Analysis: Analysis of numerical data using mathematical and statistical techniques
        Data Visualization: The graphical representation of information and data
        Decision Theory: A branch of mathematics and statistics that studies the process of making decisions

        Utility Theory: A theory that describes the level of satisfaction or usefulness that a consumer receives from consuming a good or service

        Linear Programming: A method to achieve the best outcome in a mathematical model with linear relationships
        Operations Research: The discipline of applying advanced analytical methods to help make better decisions
        Financial Mathematics: The application of mathematical methods to financial markets, risk management, and financial decision-making
        Actuarial Science: The discipline that applies mathematical and statistical methods to assess risk in insurance, finance, and other industries
        Mathematical Economics: The application of mathematical methods to represent economic theories and analyze economic problems
        Graph Theory: The study of graphs, which are mathematical structures used to model pairwise relations between objects
        Network Analysis: The study of networks, including their structure, properties, and dynamics

        Forecasting: The process of making predictions based on past and present data

        Monte Carlo Simulation: A technique used to understand the impact of risk and uncertainty in prediction and forecasting models
        Agent-Based Modeling: A computational model for simulating the actions and interactions of autonomous agents with a view to assessing their effects on the system as a whole
