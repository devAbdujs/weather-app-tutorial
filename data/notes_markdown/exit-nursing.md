 QSEN (Quality and Safety Education for Nurses): An initiative focusing on six core competencies (patient-centered care, teamwork, EBP, QI, safety, and informatics) to prepare future nurses with the knowledge and skills necessary for the healthcare system
 Incident Report: A confidential document prepared by a nurse to record the details of an unusual event, such as a patient fall or medication error, used primarily for quality improvement and risk management
 Nursing Care Plan (NCP): A formal process that identifies existing needs and potential risks, serving as a roadmap for the nursing team to ensure individualized and goal-oriented patient care
 Standardized Nursing Languages (SNL): A common language or terminology used in the nursing profession to ensure that nursing practice, interventions, and outcomes are documented consistently across different facilities
 Primary Nursing: A delivery model of care where one nurse is assigned responsibility for the total care of a specific patient throughout their hospital stay, emphasizing continuity of care and accountability
 Float Pool: A staffing strategy where a group of nurses is not assigned to a permanent unit but instead "floats" to different departments based on daily patient volume and staffing needs
 Acuity System: A method used to determine the intensity of nursing care required by a patient, which directly influences staffing ratios and the distribution of the nursing workload
 Magnet Recognition Program: A status granted by the ANCC to healthcare organizations that demonstrate excellence in nursing leadership, professional practice, and high-quality patient outcomes
 Bioethics: A field of study addressing ethical issues and dilemmas that arise in medicine and biology, providing nurses with a framework for handling complex moral questions such as end-of-life care or genetic testing
