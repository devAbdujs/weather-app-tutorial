Hydrology: The science dealing with the occurrence, circulation, distribution, and properties of water on, in, and above the Earth's surface, including the hydrologic cycle
Hydrologic Cycle: The continuous circulation of water through evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, runoff, and groundwater flow
Precipitation: Water released from the atmosphere in liquid or solid form (rain, snow, hail, sleet) that reaches the earth's surface
Evaporation: The process by which liquid water is converted to water vapor and transferred to the atmosphere from open water or soil surfaces
Transpiration: The process by which water absorbed by plant roots is released into the atmosphere as vapor through leaf stomata
Evapotranspiration: The combined loss of water from a land area through evaporation from soil/water surfaces and transpiration from plants
Infiltration: The process by which water on the ground surface enters the soil
Percolation: The downward movement of water through soil layers below the root zone toward the water table
Runoff: That portion of precipitation that flows over the land surface and eventually reaches a stream channel
Interception: Rainfall captured and temporarily stored on vegetation surfaces, part of which evaporates without reaching the ground
Catchment Area (Watershed): The total area that drains to a particular point in a stream or river
Time of Concentration: The time required for runoff to travel from the most hydraulically distant point in a watershed to the outlet
Rational Method: A method for estimating peak runoff using Q=CiA, where C is runoff coefficient, i is rainfall intensity, A is area
Runoff Coefficient: A dimensionless factor representing the fraction of rainfall that becomes surface runoff
SCS Curve Number Method: An empirical method developed by the Soil Conservation Service to estimate direct runoff from rainfall based on land use, soil type, and antecedent moisture
Unit Hydrograph: The hydrograph of direct runoff resulting from one unit of effective rainfall occurring uniformly over a basin in a unit time
Hydrograph: A graph showing the variation of discharge with time at a particular point in a river or channel
Baseflow: The sustained portion of streamflow contributed by groundwater discharge into a stream channel
Recession Curve: The declining limb of a hydrograph following the peak, representing depletion of channel and groundwater storage
Time to Peak: The time interval from the start of rainfall (or rise of hydrograph) to the occurrence of peak discharge
Flood Frequency Analysis: Statistical analysis of historical flood data to estimate the probability of occurrence of floods of various magnitudes
Return Period (Recurrence Interval): The average interval of time within which a given event is expected to be equaled or exceeded at least once
Log-Pearson Type III Distribution: A statistical distribution commonly used in flood frequency analysis to accommodate the skewness typical of flood data
IDF Curve (Intensity-Duration-Frequency): A curve relating rainfall intensity, duration, and return period, used for design storm development
Design Storm: A hypothetical rainfall event with specified intensity, duration, and return period used as design input for hydraulic structures
Alternating Block Method: A method for constructing a design storm hyetograph from IDF curve data
Isohyetal Method: A method of estimating average rainfall over an area using contour lines connecting points of equal rainfall
Thiessen Polygon Method: A method of estimating average rainfall by assigning weighted areas of influence to each rain gauge
Normal Ratio Method: A method for estimating missing rainfall data using nearby station records weighted by normal annual precipitation
Double Mass Curve: A graphical method used to check the consistency of rainfall records over time
Flood Routing: The technique of determining the timing and magnitude of a flood wave as it moves through a river reach or reservoir
Muskingum Method: A hydrologic flood routing method using a storage-discharge relationship with parameters K and X
Kinematic Wave Approximation: A simplified flood routing method that neglects inertial and pressure gradient terms in the momentum equation
Reservoir Routing: The process of determining outflow hydrograph from a reservoir given the inflow hydrograph and storage characteristics
Continuity Equation: The principle of conservation of mass applied to fluid flow, expressed as Q=A1V1=A2V2 for incompressible steady flow
Bernoulli's Equation: An energy conservation equation for fluid flow stating P/γ + V²/2g + z = constant along a streamline
Darcy-Weisbach Equation: An equation for calculating friction head loss in pipe flow, hf=f(L/D)(V²/2g)
Hazen-Williams Equation: An empirical formula for calculating friction head loss in water pipes based on a roughness coefficient C
Manning's Equation: A formula for calculating flow velocity in open channels, V=(1/n)R^(2/3)S^(1/2)
Chezy's Formula: An open channel flow formula, V=C√(RS), predecessor to Manning's equation
Reynolds Number: A dimensionless number comparing inertial to viscous forces, used to classify flow as laminar or turbulent
Froude Number: A dimensionless number comparing inertial to gravitational forces, used to classify open channel flow as subcritical or supercritical
Laminar Flow: Smooth, orderly fluid motion in parallel layers, occurring at low Reynolds numbers
Turbulent Flow: Chaotic, irregular fluid motion occurring at high Reynolds numbers
Critical Depth: The depth of flow at which specific energy is minimum for a given discharge
Specific Energy: The energy per unit weight of flowing water measured relative to the channel bed, E=y+V²/2g
Alternate Depths: The two depths (subcritical and supercritical) that share the same specific energy value for a given discharge
Hydraulic Jump: An abrupt transition from supercritical to subcritical flow accompanied by significant energy dissipation
Hydraulic Radius: The ratio of flow cross-sectional area to wetted perimeter, R=A/P
Wetted Perimeter: The length of the channel boundary in contact with flowing water
Most Economical Channel Section: A channel cross-section that minimizes wetted perimeter for a given area, maximizing hydraulic efficiency
Weir: A barrier across a channel used to measure flow rate or control upstream water level
Coefficient of Discharge (Cd): An empirical correction factor accounting for energy losses and contraction in flow measurement devices
Venturi Meter: A flow measurement device that uses a constriction in a pipe to create a pressure differential related to flow rate
Orifice: A small opening through which fluid flows, with discharge given by Q=Cd×A×√(2gh)
Torricelli's Theorem: A theorem stating the theoretical velocity of efflux through an orifice equals √(2gh)
Pitot Tube: A device used to measure local fluid velocity by measuring stagnation pressure
Notch: A weir with a specific opening shape (rectangular, triangular/V-notch, trapezoidal) used for precise flow measurement
Backwater Curve: The water surface profile resulting from an obstruction causing flow depth to increase upstream
Gradually Varied Flow: Open channel flow in which depth changes gradually along the channel length
Rapidly Varied Flow: Open channel flow in which depth changes abruptly over a short distance, such as at a hydraulic jump
Specific Discharge (Darcy Flux): The apparent velocity of groundwater flow calculated using the full cross-sectional area, q=Q/A
Seepage Velocity: The actual average velocity of water through pore spaces in an aquifer, v=q/n (n=effective porosity)
Darcy's Law: A law describing groundwater flow through porous media, Q=-KA(dh/dl)
Hydraulic Conductivity (K): A measure of a soil/rock's ability to transmit water under a hydraulic gradient
Transmissivity (T): The rate at which water moves through the full saturated thickness of an aquifer, T=Kb
Storativity (Storage Coefficient, S): The volume of water released from storage per unit area per unit decline in head in a confined aquifer
Specific Yield: The volume of water released from storage per unit area per unit decline in water table in an unconfined aquifer
Specific Retention: The water held in soil pores against gravity drainage, complementary to specific yield (Porosity=Specific Yield+Specific Retention)
Porosity: The ratio of void space to total volume of a soil or rock material
Effective Porosity: The portion of total porosity consisting of interconnected pores through which water can actually flow
Confined Aquifer: An aquifer bounded above and below by impermeable layers, water under pressure
Unconfined Aquifer: An aquifer with a water table as its upper boundary, directly connected to the atmosphere
Aquitard: A geological layer with low but non-zero permeability that retards but does not completely prevent water movement
Aquiclude: An essentially impermeable geological layer that prevents significant water movement
Aquifer Anisotropy: The directional dependence of hydraulic conductivity within an aquifer
Cone of Depression: The radially symmetric depression in the water table or potentiometric surface around a pumping well
Theis Equation: The non-equilibrium equation describing transient drawdown in a confined aquifer due to pumping
Cooper-Jacob Method: A simplified approximation of the Theis equation used to analyze pumping test drawdown data
Pumping Test (Aquifer Test): A controlled field test to determine aquifer hydraulic properties (T and S) by measuring drawdown response
Specific Capacity: The discharge rate per unit drawdown of a well, an indicator of well performance
Safe Yield (Sustainable Yield): The maximum rate of groundwater withdrawal sustainable without long-term depletion or adverse effects
Groundwater Mining: Extraction of groundwater exceeding the natural recharge rate, depleting aquifer storage
Land Subsidence: Gradual sinking of land surface caused by compaction of aquifer materials from excessive groundwater extraction
Ghyben-Herzberg Relationship: An equation estimating the depth of the freshwater-saltwater interface below sea level in coastal aquifers
Saltwater Intrusion: The landward migration of saline water into a freshwater aquifer, often due to excessive pumping
Conjunctive Use: The coordinated management of surface water and groundwater resources together
Recharge: The process by which water enters and replenishes an aquifer, naturally or artificially
Tracer Test: A groundwater investigation method using injected substances to determine flow paths and travel times
Isotope Hydrology: The use of stable or radioactive isotopes as natural tracers in water resources investigations
Dam: A barrier constructed to impound water for storage, flood control, irrigation, or power generation
Gravity Dam: A dam that resists water pressure primarily through its own weight
Arch Dam: A dam that transfers water load horizontally into canyon abutments through arch action
Buttress Dam: A dam supported by a series of buttresses on the downstream side
Embankment (Earth) Dam: A dam constructed of compacted soil or rock fill materials
Spillway: A structure designed to safely release excess reservoir water during floods
Ogee Spillway: A curved-profile spillway crest shaped to match the natural underside of a free-falling water jet
Siphon Spillway: A spillway that automatically discharges excess water using siphonic action
Energy Dissipator (Stilling Basin): A structure designed to dissipate the high kinetic energy of spillway discharge, typically via a hydraulic jump
Freeboard: The vertical distance between maximum water level and the top of a dam, levee, or channel
Probable Maximum Flood (PMF): The most extreme flood reasonably possible based on the most severe combination of meteorological conditions
Design Flood: The flood used as the basis for normal hydraulic design of a structure
Check Flood: A larger flood used to verify the structural safety of a dam beyond the design flood
Piping (Internal Erosion): Progressive erosion of seepage paths through an embankment dam or foundation, a major dam failure mechanism
Reservoir Mass Curve (Rippl Diagram): A graphical method plotting cumulative inflow versus time used to determine required reservoir storage capacity
Firm Yield (Safe Yield of Reservoir): The maximum quantity of water reliably suppliable from a reservoir during the most critical drought period
Gross Storage Capacity: The total volume of a reservoir from streambed to maximum operating level
Live (Useful) Storage: The portion of reservoir storage available for operational use, excluding dead storage
Dead Storage: The reservoir volume below minimum operating level, reserved for sediment accumulation
Flow Duration Curve: A graph showing the percentage of time that streamflow equals or exceeds various discharge values
Water Budget (Water Balance): An accounting of all water inflows, outflows, and storage changes in a hydrologic system based on mass conservation
Consumptive Use: Water removed from the immediate source without return, primarily through evapotranspiration
Return Flow: The portion of diverted water not consumed, returning to surface water or groundwater
Duty of Water: The area of land irrigable by a unit discharge flowing continuously for the entire crop base period
Delta: The total depth of water required by a crop during its entire growth period
Consumptive Irrigation Requirement: The crop water need (evapotranspiration) not met by effective rainfall
Gross Irrigation Requirement: The net irrigation requirement divided by application efficiency
Application Efficiency: The fraction of applied irrigation water actually stored in the root zone for plant use
Crop Coefficient (Kc): A factor applied to reference evapotranspiration to estimate actual crop water use at a given growth stage
Reference Evapotranspiration (ET0): The evapotranspiration rate from a standardized reference surface, used as a baseline for crop water calculations
Penman-Monteith Equation: A physically-based method for estimating evapotranspiration combining energy balance and aerodynamic terms
Field Capacity: The amount of soil moisture held after excess water has drained and downward movement has decreased
Permanent Wilting Point: The minimum soil moisture content below which plants cannot extract sufficient water and wilt permanently
Available Water Capacity: The soil moisture range between field capacity and permanent wilting point, available for plant use
Lacey's Regime Theory: A theory for designing stable alluvial irrigation channel dimensions based on discharge and silt factor
Self-Cleansing Velocity: The minimum flow velocity required to prevent sediment deposition in a channel or sewer
Scouring Velocity: The maximum permissible velocity to prevent erosion of channel bed and banks
Sediment Transport: The movement of solid particles by flowing water, encompassing bedload and suspended load
Shields Parameter: A dimensionless number used to determine the threshold condition for initiation of sediment particle movement
Channel Braiding: A river pattern of multiple shifting channels separated by sediment bars, occurring under high sediment load conditions
Channel Meandering: The natural sinuous, curving pattern that develops in alluvial river channels over time
Pier Scour: Localized streambed erosion around a bridge pier foundation due to flow acceleration and vortices
Riprap: Graded rock armor used to protect channel banks and structures from erosion
Channel Degradation: Progressive lowering of a channel bed, often occurring downstream of a dam due to sediment-deficient flow
Wave Celerity: The speed at which a disturbance (flood wave, surge) propagates through a channel, distinct from particle velocity
Water Hammer: A pressure surge caused by sudden changes in flow velocity, such as rapid valve closure
Joukowsky Equation: An equation relating water hammer pressure surge to fluid density, wave celerity, and velocity change, ΔP=ρcΔV
Surge Tank: A structure that absorbs pressure surges and stabilizes flow in a pipeline system
Cavitation: The formation and violent collapse of vapor bubbles due to localized pressure dropping below vapor pressure
NPSH (Net Positive Suction Head): The margin between suction pressure and vapor pressure available to prevent pump cavitation
Specific Speed: A pump parameter combining flow, head, and rotational speed used to classify and select pump impeller type
Pump Affinity Laws: Relationships describing how pump flow, head, and power change with rotational speed
Priming: Filling a centrifugal pump casing and suction line with liquid to remove air before startup
Thrust Block: A structure providing bearing resistance against unbalanced forces at pipe bends and fittings
Minor Losses: Head losses in pipe flow due to fittings, bends, and valves, expressed as hL=K(V²/2g)
Hydraulic Grade Line (HGL): A line representing pressure head plus elevation head along a pipe or channel
Energy Grade Line (EGL): A line representing total energy (pressure, velocity, and elevation head) along a flow path
Hardy Cross Method: An iterative method for analyzing flow distribution in pipe networks
Pipe Network Analysis: The process of determining flow rates and pressures throughout an interconnected pipe system
Non-Revenue Water (NRW): Water produced but lost before reaching customers, generating no revenue
Real (Physical) Losses: Water lost from a distribution system through leaks, breaks, or overflows
Apparent Losses: Water delivered to customers but unaccounted for in revenue, due to meter error or theft
Water Age: The time water spends in a distribution system from treatment to the point of use
Pressure Reducing Valve (PRV): A valve that reduces excessive pressure to a safe operating range in elevation-varied systems
BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand): The amount of dissolved oxygen consumed by microorganisms decomposing organic matter over 5 days
COD (Chemical Oxygen Demand): The amount of oxygen required to chemically oxidize organic and inorganic matter in water
Dissolved Oxygen (DO): The concentration of oxygen dissolved in water, essential for aquatic life
Streeter-Phelps Equation: A model describing the dissolved oxygen sag curve downstream of an organic pollution source
Eutrophication: Excessive nutrient enrichment of a water body leading to algal blooms and oxygen depletion
Turbidity: The cloudiness of water caused by suspended particles, measured in NTU
Hardness: Water quality measure caused by dissolved calcium and magnesium ions
Alkalinity: The buffering capacity of water against pH changes, primarily from bicarbonate and carbonate
Coagulation: The chemical process of destabilizing colloidal particles in water using coagulants like alum
Flocculation: The process of aggregating destabilized particles into larger, settleable flocs through gentle mixing
Velocity Gradient (G value): A quantifiable measure of mixing intensity in flocculation, related to power input and viscosity
Sedimentation: The settling of suspended particles from water under the influence of gravity
Detention Time: The average time water spends in a treatment tank or basin, calculated as volume divided by flow rate
Rapid Sand Filter: A filtration unit operating at high filtration rates, requiring coagulation pretreatment
Slow Sand Filter: A filtration unit relying on a biological schmutzdecke layer, operating without chemical pretreatment
Backwashing: Reversing flow through a filter at high velocity to remove accumulated solids and restore capacity
Disinfection: The process of inactivating pathogenic microorganisms in water, commonly via chlorination
CT Value: The product of disinfectant concentration and contact time, used to ensure adequate pathogen inactivation
Residual Chlorine: The chlorine remaining in water after disinfection, providing ongoing protection in distribution
Activated Sludge Process: A biological wastewater treatment process using suspended microorganisms to degrade organic matter
Trickling Filter: A fixed-film biological treatment process where wastewater trickles over a media bed
Return Activated Sludge (RAS): Settled biological solids recycled from the clarifier back to the aeration tank
Mixed Liquor Suspended Solids (MLSS): The concentration of suspended solids in the aeration tank of an activated sludge system
Sludge Digestion: Biological stabilization of sludge to reduce volume, pathogens, and odor
Grit Chamber: A unit process removing heavy inorganic solids like sand and gravel through gravity settling
Primary Treatment: Physical wastewater treatment processes removing settleable and floating solids
Secondary Treatment: Biological wastewater treatment processes removing dissolved and colloidal organic matter
Tertiary Treatment: Advanced wastewater treatment achieving higher effluent quality beyond secondary treatment
Membrane Bioreactor (MBR): A wastewater treatment technology combining biological treatment with membrane filtration
Combined Sewer System: A sewer system carrying both sanitary sewage and stormwater in a single pipe
Separate Sewer System: A sewer system using distinct pipes for sanitary sewage and stormwater
Combined Sewer Overflow (CSO): The discharge of untreated combined sewage during heavy rainfall when capacity is exceeded
Infiltration (Sewer): Groundwater entering a sewer system through defective pipes, joints, or manholes
Inflow (Sewer): Stormwater entering a sanitary sewer through direct connections like roof drains
Manhole: An access structure for inspection, cleaning, and maintenance of sewer pipes
Invert Level: The lowest internal point of a pipe's cross-section, governing slope and gravity flow
Detention Basin: A stormwater facility temporarily storing runoff and releasing it at a controlled rate
Retention Basin (Wet Pond): A stormwater facility maintaining a permanent pool providing extended water quality treatment
Green Infrastructure: Stormwater management approaches mimicking natural hydrologic processes, such as bioswales and permeable pavement
First Flush: The phenomenon of disproportionately higher pollutant concentration in the initial portion of stormwater runoff
Level of Service (LOS): A defined performance standard a drainage system is intended to achieve
Time of Concentration: See above; critical parameter for Rational Method design storm duration
Culvert: A structure conveying water under a roadway, embankment, or other obstruction
Inlet Control: Culvert flow condition where capacity is limited by entrance geometry and headwater depth
Outlet Control: Culvert flow condition where capacity is limited by downstream tailwater and barrel friction losses
Fish Ladder (Fishway): A structure allowing migratory fish to pass upstream around a dam or barrier
Environmental Flow: The minimum flow and natural variability maintained in a river to support ecosystem health
Water Rights (Riparian/Prior Appropriation): Legal doctrines governing entitlement to use water from a source
Integrated Water Resources Management (IWRM): A coordinated approach managing water, land, and related resources for equitable and sustainable benefit
Non-Stationarity: The recognition that hydrologic statistics may shift over time due to climate or land use change
Sensitivity Analysis: Systematic evaluation of how model outputs change in response to variations in input parameters
Asset Management: Systematic infrastructure condition assessment and risk-based prioritization of investment decisions
Water Footprint: The total volume of water consumed or polluted to produce goods and services, including virtual water
Desalination (Reverse Osmosis): A membrane process removing dissolved salts from seawater or brackish water to produce potable water
Water Reuse: The beneficial use of treated wastewater effluent for irrigation, industrial, or potable applications
SCADA: Supervisory control and data acquisition systems used for monitoring and control of water infrastructure
