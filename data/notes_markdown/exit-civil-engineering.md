 X-band Radar: A radar frequency used in construction for soil testing, detecting subsurface features, or mapping foundations. 
 Xenon Exposure Testing: A type of material testing in which construction materials are exposed to simulated sunlight (Xenon lights) to assess their longevity and durability in real-world conditions. 
 Yield Strength: The stress at which a material starts to deform plastically and will not return to its original shape when the load is removed. 
 Yoke: A structural component used to connect or secure elements of a system, commonly used in bridge design or structural frames. 
 Yellow Sand: A term referring to fine sand often used in construction materials or for creating backfill in foundations.

 Y-bolt: A type of bolt with a "Y" shape, used in various construction applications to connect structural elements securely. 
 Yellow River: A significant river in China, often used as a reference point in hydrology and river management projects. 
 Zero Load: The condition in which a structure is not subjected to any external load or forces, often used as a baseline in structural analysis. 
 Zoning: The process of dividing land into zones for specific uses such as residential, commercial, industrial, and agricultural, a key aspect of urban planning. 
 Z-Shape Beam: A beam with a "Z" cross-section, used in specialized applications for its strength and efficiency in resisting bending. 
 Zinc Coating: The process of coating steel or iron with a layer of zinc to prevent corrosion, commonly known as galvanization. 
 Zero Energy Building: A building designed to produce as much energy as it consumes over a year, a key concept in sustainable architecture and construction.
