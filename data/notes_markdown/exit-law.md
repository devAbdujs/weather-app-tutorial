Law encompasses a diverse range of subjects, each playing a crucial role in governing society and maintaining order. Here's a more detailed exploration:
Private Law: Private law focuses on legal relationships between individuals and entities, covering areas such as contracts, property rights, family law, business organization, and succession. It delves into the rules and principles that govern these relationships, ensuring fairness and accountability
Public Law: Public law deals with the relationship between individuals and the state, including constitutional law, criminal law, tax law, public international law, and human rights law. It establishes the framework for government authority, individual rights, and international cooperation
Laws of Procedure and Skills: This area of law provides students with practical knowledge and skills essential for legal practice. It includes civil and criminal procedure, evidence gathering, legal research, writing, and advocacy techniques. Proficiency in these areas is vital for effective legal representation
Miscellaneous Courses: These courses offer a broader perspective on legal issues, covering topics such as employment law, legal ethics, jurisprudence, and other specialized areas. They encourage critical thinking and interdisciplinary approaches to legal analysis
Law of Contracts: Contracts are fundamental to private law, governing agreements between parties and establishing rights and obligations. Key concepts include offer, acceptance, consideration, and breach of contract
 Property Law: Property law deals with the rights and responsibilities associated with ownership, possession, and use of property. It covers real property (land and buildings) and personal property (movable assets)
 Family Law: Family law addresses legal matters related to familial relationships, including marriage, divorce, child custody, adoption, and inheritance rights. It aims to protect the interests of family members and promote stability and welfare
 Law of Business Organizations: This area of law governs the formation, operation, and dissolution of businesses, including corporations, partnerships, and limited liability companies. It regulates corporate governance, shareholder rights, and business transactions
 Law of Succession: The law of succession governs the distribution of a person's estate after death, including inheritance rights, wills, trusts, and estate administration. It ensures orderly transfer of assets and fulfillment of testamentary wishes
Public Law Subjects: Constitutional Law: Constitutional law establishes the framework of government, outlining the powers and limitations of governmental institutions. It protects fundamental rights and liberties and ensures the rule of law
 Criminal Law: Criminal law defines offenses against the state and prescribes punishments for unlawful conduct. It aims to deter crime, rehabilitate offenders, and protect public safety and order
 Tax Law: Tax law governs the imposition and collection of taxes by government authorities. It regulates taxpayer rights and responsibilities, tax planning, and compliance with tax laws
 Public International Law: Public international law governs relations between sovereign states and international organizations. It includes treaties, customary practices, diplomatic relations, and the resolution of international disputes
 Human Rights Law: Human rights law protects the inherent dignity and freedoms of individuals, prohibiting discrimination, torture, and other human rights abuses. It promotes equality, justice, and respect for human dignity
 Law of Civil Procedure: Civil procedure sets out the rules and procedures for resolving disputes between private parties through the court system. It includes pleadings, motions, discovery, trial, and appeal processes
 Law of Criminal Procedure: Criminal procedure governs the investigation and prosecution of criminal offenses, ensuring procedural fairness, due process, and protection of defendants' rights
 Law of Evidence: The law of evidence establishes the rules for the admission and presentation of evidence in legal proceedings. It governs the relevance, admissibility, and weight of evidence presented in court
 Employment Law: Employment law regulates the relationship between employers and employees, covering issues such as wages, hours, discrimination, harassment, and workplace safety
 Legal Ethics: Legal ethics sets out the ethical standards and professional responsibilities that lawyers must uphold in their practice. It includes confidentiality, conflicts of interest, client representation, and honesty
 Jurisprudence: Jurisprudence explores the philosophical foundations of law, examining concepts such as justice, rights, morality, and the nature of legal reasoning. It encourages critical analysis and reflection on the nature and purpose of law
Affirm: To confirm a judgement on appeal, or uphold it. Meaning, the appellate court confirms that the lower court ruled correctly
Appellate Court: This is the type of court that hears appeals. This means the party that lost at the lower court level appealed to a higher court. The cases you typically read in law school are at the appellate court level
BlueBook: This is the book most legal writing courses, law firms, judges, and law journals and law reviews use for uniform citation. You might hear people talk about “bluebooking”, which generally means checking citations
Case Law: The law derived from a collection of cases. Essentially, judges will write opinions, and that creates caw law, or precedent. Case law can be common law, or, it can be used to explain and supplement statutory law
Common Law: The body of law derived from cases, rather than a statute
Moot Court: A fictitious court, usually held in a law school setting, to argue “moot”, or hypothetical, cases. These cases are usually at the appellate level
Petitioner: One who appeals from a judgment
Administrative Law: The body of law that governs the activities of administrative agencies of government, including rule-making, adjudication, and enforcement of a specific regulatory agenda
Environmental Law: Legal principles and regulations that govern the interaction between human activity and the natural environment, aiming to protect and conserve natural resources
Intellectual Property Law: The area of law that deals with protecting the rights of those who create original works, including patents, trademarks, copyrights, and trade secrets
Torts Law: The body of law that addresses and provides remedies for civil wrongs not arising out of contractual obligations. It includes negligence, intentional torts, and strict liability
Corporate Law: The study of how shareholders, directors, employees, creditors, and other stakeholders interact under the rules and regulations of the corporations and securities law
Bankruptcy Law: Legal procedures for dealing with debt problems of individuals and businesses, including the process of filing for bankruptcy and the rights of creditors and debtors
International Trade Law: The body of rules and regulations that governs trade between countries, including international agreements, tariffs, and trade barriers
Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR): A set of practices and techniques aimed at enabling the resolution of conflicts and disputes outside traditional judicial processes, including arbitration and mediation
Cyber Law: The area of law that deals with the Internet, encompassing issues such as online privacy, digital contracts, intellectual property, and e-commerce
Insurance Law: The practice of law surrounding insurance, including insurance policies, claims, and the regulation of the insurance industry
Maritime Law: The branch of law that deals with issues and disputes related to maritime activities, including shipping, navigation, and marine resources
Taxation Law: Legal regulations and principles governing the assessment and collection of taxes by authorities, and the rights and obligations of taxpayers
