 Zero Inventory: A supply chain strategy where a company aims to minimize inventory levels to reduce storage costs and increase efficiency, often relying on just-in-time inventory systems.Servant Leadership: A leadership philosophy in which the main goal of the leader is to serve, prioritizing the growth and well-being of people and the communities to which they belong over the exercise of power or self-interest
 Leader-Member Exchange (LMX) Theory: A relationship-based approach to leadership that focuses on the two-way relationship between managers and each individual subordinate, suggesting that leaders develop different relationship qualities with different team members
 Bounded Rationality: The idea that in decision-making, individuals are limited by the information they have, the cognitive limitations of their minds, and the finite amount of time they have to make a decision, leading to 'satisficing' rather than optimizing
 Ishikawa Diagram (Fishbone Diagram): A visualization tool used in quality management to categorize the potential causes of a problem or 'effect' in order to identify its root causes
Pareto Principle (80/20 Rule): The observation that roughly 80% of effects come from 20% of the causes; in management, this is often used to identify the vital few tasks or customers that produce the most significant results
 Ansoff Matrix: A strategic planning tool that provides a framework to help executives, senior managers, and marketers devise strategies for future growth through market penetration, market development, product development, or diversification
PESTEL Analysis: A framework used by marketers and managers to monitor and analyze the macro-environmental factors that have an impact on an organization, standing for Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, and Legal factors 
Theory of Constraints (TOC): A management paradigm that views any manageable system as being limited in achieving more of its goals by a very small number of constraints (bottlenecks), focusing efforts on improving that specific constraint
 Double-Loop Learning: An educational concept and management theory that involves not just fixing a mistake (single-loop), but also questioning the underlying values, assumptions, and policies that led to the mistake in the first place
 Six Thinking Hats: A tool for group discussion and individual thinking developed by Edward de Bono, involving six colored hats that represent different perspectives—logic, emotion, caution, optimism, creativity, and management
 Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs: A motivational theory in psychology comprising a five-tier model of human needs, often depicted as hierarchical levels within a pyramid, used by managers to understand employee motivation
Herzberg’s Two-Factor Theory: A theory that states there are certain factors in the workplace that cause job satisfaction (motivators) while a separate set of factors cause dissatisfaction (hygiene factors)
 McKinsey 7S Framework: A model used to analyze an organization's organizational design by looking at 7 key internal elements: strategy, structure, systems, shared values, style, staff, and skills, to see if they are aligned
 VRIO Framework: A strategic tool used to analyze an organization's internal resources and capabilities to determine their competitive potential based on whether they are Valuable, Rare, Inimitable, and Organized
 Psychological Safety: The shared belief held by members of a team that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking, allowing employees to speak up with ideas, questions, or concerns without fear of punishment or humiliation
 Management by Wandering Around (MBWA): An unstructured management style that involves managers wandering through the workplace, at random, to check with employees, equipment, or on the status of ongoing work
 Force Field Analysis: A central tool in social science for understanding and managing organizational change by identifying the 'driving forces' that push for change and the 'restraining forces' that act against it
Organizational Silos: A mindset or situation where certain departments or sectors do not wish to share information or knowledge with others in the same company, reducing efficiency and morale
 Emotional Labor: The process of managing feelings and expressions to fulfill the emotional requirements of a job, typically requiring employees to maintain a specific facial expression or tone during interactions
 Span of Control: The number of subordinates a supervisor can effectively manage, which depends on the complexity of the work and the manager's ability to delegate
