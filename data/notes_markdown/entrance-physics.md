Acceleration: The rate of change of velocity of an object with respect to time
Atom: The smallest unit of an element, consisting of a nucleus surrounded by electrons
Ampere: The unit of electric current, defined as the flow of one coulomb of charge per second
Angular Momentum: The rotational equivalent of linear momentum, a measure of an object's rotational motion
Absorption Spectrum: The spectrum of absorbed light by a substance, which corresponds to the wavelengths that are absorbed during excitation
Bernoulli's Principle: A principle in fluid dynamics stating that an increase in the speed of a fluid occurs simultaneously with a decrease in pressure
Bolt's Law: A law in fluid mechanics stating that the pressure exerted by a fluid is proportional to the density of the fluid and the velocity of flow
Brownian Motion: The random motion of particles suspended in a fluid, resulting from collisions with molecules of the surrounding medium
Binding Energy: The energy required to disassemble a system into its individual components, typically used in nuclear physics
Black Hole: A region of space where the gravitational pull is so strong that not even light can escape
Capacitance: The ability of a system to store electric charge, measured in farads
Coulomb's Law: A fundamental principle that defines the electrostatic force between two charged objects
Charge: A fundamental property of matter that causes it to experience a force when placed in an electric and magnetic field
Centripetal Force: The force required to keep an object moving in a circular path, directed toward the center of the rotation
Conservation of Energy: The law stating that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another
Conductivity: The ability of a material to allow the flow of electric current or heat
Centrifugal Force: The apparent force that draws a rotating object away from the center of rotation, often perceived as a reaction to centripetal force
Capacitor: An electrical component that stores electrical energy in an electric field
Chemical Potential Energy: The energy stored in the chemical bonds of molecules, released during a chemical reaction
Coulomb: A unit of electric charge, representing the quantity of charge transferred by a current of one ampere in one second
Displacement: A vector quantity that refers to the change in the position of an object
Doppler Effect: The change in frequency or wavelength of a wave as observed from a moving observer relative to the source of the wave
Density: The mass per unit volume of a substance, typically expressed in kilograms per cubic meter
Direct Current (DC): An electric current that flows in one direction only
Diffraction: The bending of waves around obstacles or through openings, leading to interference patterns
Dark Matter: A type of matter inferred from its gravitational effects on visible matter, but which does not emit or interact with electromagnetic radiation
Electric Field: A vector field that represents the force per unit charge exerted on a test charge at any point in space
Electromagnetic Wave: A wave of electric and magnetic fields that propagate through space, such as light waves
Electromagnetic Spectrum: The range of all electromagnetic frequencies, from radio waves to gamma rays
Energy Quantum: The minimum amount of energy that can be gained or lost by a system, often described in terms of photons or discrete packets of energy
Electric Potential: The electric potential energy per unit charge at a specific point in space
Faraday's Law: A fundamental principle that describes how a time-varying magnetic field induces an electromotive force in a conductor
Friction: The resistive force that opposes the motion of an object, caused by the interaction of its surface with another surface
Fermion: A class of subatomic particles that obey the Pauli exclusion principle, including electrons, protons, and neutrons
Force: An interaction that changes the motion of an object, typically expressed as mass times acceleration (F=ma)
Frequency: The number of complete cycles of a periodic wave that occur per unit of time, usually measured in hertz (Hz)
Gravitational Potential Energy: The energy possessed by an object due to its position in a gravitational field
Gravitation: The force of attraction between two masses, typically described by Newton's law of gravitation
Gyroscope: A device used to measure or maintain orientation, based on the principles of angular momentum
Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle: A fundamental limit in quantum mechanics stating that the position and momentum of a particle cannot both be precisely determined at the same time
Hertz: The unit of frequency, defined as one cycle per second
Heat Capacity: The amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of a substance by one degree Celsius
Holography: A technique for creating three-dimensional images using light interference and diffraction patterns
Inertia: The tendency of an object to resist changes in its state of motion, directly related to its mass
Induction: The process by which a changing magnetic field generates an electromotive force (EMF) in a conductor
Intensity: The power transferred per unit area, typically used to describe the power of waves or light
Impulse: The product of a force and the time it acts on an object, equal to the change in momentum of the object
Ionization: The process by which an atom or molecule gains or loses electrons, resulting in a charged ion
Isotopes: Atoms of the same element that have different numbers of neutrons, resulting in different atomic masses
Inelastic Collision: A type of collision where kinetic energy is not conserved, although momentum is conserved
Joule: A unit of energy in the International System of Units (SI), equivalent to the energy expended when applying a force of one newton over a distance of one meter
Kinetic Energy: The energy possessed by an object due to its motion, calculated as ½ mv²
Kinetic Theory of Gases: A theory that describes the behavior of gases in terms of the motion of their particles
Kelvin: The SI unit of temperature, measured from absolute zero
Laws of Thermodynamics: A set of laws governing the behavior of energy and heat transfer in physical systems
Lenz's Law: A principle stating that the direction of an induced current will oppose the change in magnetic flux that caused it
Light Year: The distance that light travels in one year, approximately 9.46 trillion kilometers
Lens: A transparent optical device used to focus or disperse light
Lepton: A class of elementary particles that includes electrons, neutrinos, and their antiparticles
Lorentz Force: The force exerted on a charged particle moving through an electromagnetic field
Magnetic Field: A vector field that describes the magnetic influence of electrical charges and currents
Magnetic Flux: A measure of the total magnetic field passing through a given area
Mass: A measure of the amount of matter in an object, typically measured in kilograms
Momentum: The product of an object's mass and velocity, a measure of its motion
Newton's Laws of Motion: Three fundamental laws that describe the relationship between an object’s motion and the forces acting upon it
Optics: The study of light and its interactions with matter, including reflection, refraction, and diffraction
Oscillation: The repetitive variation of a system about a central point, often seen in mechanical and electromagnetic systems
Ohm's Law: A principle stating that the current through a conductor between two points is directly proportional to the voltage and inversely proportional to the resistance
Photon: A quantum of light or electromagnetic radiation, having zero rest mass and carrying energy proportional to its frequency
Power: The rate at which work is done or energy is transferred, measured in watts
Potential Energy: The stored energy of an object due to its position in a force field, such as a gravitational or electric field
Pressure: The force per unit area applied to the surface of an object
Quantum Mechanics: The branch of physics that describes the behavior of particles at very small scales, often involving probabilistic behavior
Quantum Tunneling: A phenomenon in quantum mechanics where particles pass through a barrier that they classically could not
Refraction: The bending of light as it passes from one medium to another due to a change in its speed
Resistance: The opposition to the flow of electric current, typically measured in ohms
Resonance: The phenomenon that occurs when an object vibrates at its natural frequency due to an external force
Scientific Method: A systematic approach to investigation and experimentation, involving observation, hypothesis, experimentation, and conclusion
Speed of Light: The constant speed at which light travels in a vacuum, approximately 3.00 x 10⁸ meters per second
Superconductivity: A phenomenon in which a material, at low temperatures, exhibits zero electrical resistance
Wavefunction: A mathematical function that describes the quantum state of a system, such as a particle or field
Work: The energy transferred to or from an object via the application of force along a displacement
Wavelength: The distance between two consecutive peaks or troughs in a wave
X-rays: High-energy electromagnetic waves with wavelengths shorter than ultraviolet light, used in medical imaging
Young's Modulus: A measure of the stiffness of a material, defined as the ratio of stress to strain
Zero Point Energy: The lowest possible energy that a quantum mechanical system may have, even in its ground state
