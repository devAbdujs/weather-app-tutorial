Aggregate Demand: The total quantity of goods and services demanded across all levels of an economy at a particular price level
Arbitrage: The simultaneous purchase and sale of an asset to profit from a difference in the price in different markets
Automatic Stabilizers: Economic policies and programs that automatically help stabilize an economy, such as unemployment insurance
Absolute Advantage: The ability of a party to produce a greater quantity of a good or service than competitors using the same amount of resources
Adverse Selection: A market process in which buyers and sellers have asymmetric information, leading to undesirable market outcomes
Balance of Payments: A record of all economic transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world
Budget Deficit: A situation in which government expenditures exceed revenue
Bond Market: The financial market in which participants can issue new debt or buy and sell debt securities
Boom and Bust Cycle: A process of economic expansion and contraction that occurs repeatedly
Barriers to Entry: Obstacles that make it difficult for new competitors to enter a market
Comparative Advantage: The ability of a country to produce a good at a lower opportunity cost than another country
Consumer Surplus: The difference between the maximum amount a consumer is willing to pay and the actual amount paid
Cost-Push Inflation: Inflation caused by an increase in prices of inputs like labour, raw materials, etc.
Crowding Out: A situation where increased government spending leads to a reduction in private sector investment
Current Account: The part of the balance of payments that records a nation's exports and imports of goods and services
Demand Elasticity: A measure of how much the quantity demanded of a good responds to a change in price
Deadweight Loss: A loss of economic efficiency that occurs when equilibrium is not achieved
Deflationary Spiral: A downward cycle where lower prices lead to lower production, wages, demand, and further decreases in prices
Depreciation (Currency): A decrease in the value of a country's currency relative to another currency
Disposable Income: Income remaining after deduction of taxes and social security charges, available to be spent or saved
Economic Growth: An increase in the amount of goods and services produced per head of the population over a period of time
Economies of Scale: Cost advantages reaped by companies when production becomes efficient
Elasticity of Supply: A measure of how much the quantity supplied of a good responds to a change in the price
Exchange Rate Mechanism: A system introduced by European countries to reduce exchange rate variability
Externalities: Costs or benefits of economic activities that are not reflected in market prices
Fiscal Policy: The use of government revenue collection and expenditure to influence the economy
Fixed Exchange Rate: A regime where the currency's value is tied to another currency or basket of currencies
Foreign Direct Investment (FDI): Investment made by a firm or individual in one country into business interests located in another country
Fringe Benefits: Extra benefits supplementing an employee's salary, like company cars, pensions, etc.
Free Trade Agreement: A pact between two or more nations to reduce barriers to imports and exports among them
Gross Domestic Product (GDP): The total value of goods produced and services provided in a country during one year
Gini Coefficient: A statistical measure of income or wealth distribution among a population
Green Economics: An area of economics focused on environmental health and sustainable development
Game Theory: A theoretical framework for conceiving social situations among competing players
Globalization: The process by which businesses or other organizations develop international influence or start operating on an international scale
Human Capital: The economic value of a worker's experience and skills
Hyperinflation: Extremely rapid or out of control inflation
Horizontal Integration: A strategy where a company acquires or merges with a similar company in the same industry
Hot Money: Capital that moves between economies to profit from differences in interest rates and/or anticipated exchange rate shifts
Hedging: An investment strategy employed to offset potential losses in other investments
Income Effect: The change in consumption patterns due to a change in purchasing power
Income Elasticity of Demand: A measure of how much the quantity demanded of a good responds to a change in consumers' income
Index Number: A statistical measure designed to show changes in a variable or group of related variables over time
Import Quota: A limit on the amount of a good that can be imported into a country
Inflation Targeting: A monetary policy where a central bank sets a specific inflation rate as its goal
Joint Venture: A business entity created by two or more parties, generally characterized by shared ownership
J-Curve Effect: A phenomenon where a country's trade deficit worsens following a devaluation before it improves
Just-in-Time Production: A strategy that strives to improve a business's return on investment by reducing in-process inventory
Jobless Recovery: An economic recovery during which the economy improves while the unemployment rate remains high
Judicial Review (Economics): The process of courts examining the actions of public agencies for their economic legality
Keynesian Economics: A theory that government intervention is necessary to ensure economic growth and stability
Kaldor-Hicks Efficiency: A situation where those that are made better off could in theory compensate those that are made worse off
Knowledge Economy: An economy in which growth is dependent on the quantity, quality, and accessibility of information
Kuznets Curve: A graphical representation of the relationship between economic development and income inequality
Kickback: A payment made to someone who has facilitated a transaction or appointment illicitly
Liquidity Trap: A situation where monetary policy becomes ineffective because people hoard cash instead of spending or investing it
Laffer Curve: A graphical representation showing the relationship between tax rates and tax revenue
Labor Force Participation Rate: The percentage of the working-age population that is either working or actively looking for work
Lorenz Curve: A graphical representation of income or wealth distribution
Law of Diminishing Returns: The principle that as investment in a single factor of production increases, the rate of return after some point will decrease
Monetary Policy: The process by which a monetary authority controls the money supply, often targeting an inflation rate or interest rate
Moral Hazard: A situation where one party is able to take risks because it does not have to bear the full consequences
Marginal Cost: The cost of producing one additional unit of a good
Market Failure: A situation in which the allocation of goods and services is not efficient
Multiplier Effect: The proportional amount of increase in final income that results from an injection of spending
Nash Equilibrium: A situation in a game where no player can gain by unilaterally changing strategies if the strategies of the others remain unchanged
Net National Product (NNP): The market value of a nation's goods and services minus depreciation
Nominal GDP: The gross domestic product evaluated at current market prices
Non-Tariff Barriers: Trade barriers that restrict imports or exports of goods or services through mechanisms other than tariffs
Natural Monopoly: A type of monopoly that exists due to the high fixed costs or startup costs of operating a business in a specific industry
Opportunity Cost: The loss of potential gain from other alternatives when one alternative is chosen
Oligopoly: A market structure in which a small number of firms has the large majority of market share
Open Economy: An economy that has external interactions in terms of trade, investment, and finance
Overhead Costs: Ongoing expenses not directly attributed to creating a product or service
Output Gap: The difference between the actual output of an economy and its potential output
Price Discrimination: The practice of selling the same product at different prices to different customers
Productivity: The efficiency with which output is produced by using inputs
Purchasing Power Parity (PPP): A theory that states exchange rates should adjust so that identical goods cost the same in different countries
Public Goods: Goods that are non-excludable and non-rivalrous, meaning they are available to all without diminishing the supply
Phillips Curve: A curve that shows the inverse relationship between the rate of unemployment and the rate of inflation
Quantity Theory of Money: The theory that the general price level of goods and services is directly proportional to the amount of money in circulation
Quotas (Economics): A government-imposed trade restriction that limits the number or monetary value of goods that can be imported or exported
Quality Adjusted Life Year (QALY): A measure used to assess the value for money of medical interventions
Quasi-Public Goods: Goods that have characteristics of both private and public goods
Quota Rent: The extra profits that producers make when supply is artificially limited by an import quota
Recession: A significant decline in economic activity across the economy lasting more than a few months
Return on Investment (ROI): A measure used to evaluate the efficiency or profitability of an investment
Regressive Tax: A tax applied uniformly, taking a larger percentage of income from low-income earners than from high-income earners
Ricardian Equivalence: The theory that consumers are forward-looking and so internalize the government's budget constraint
Reserve Requirement: The minimum amount of reserves a bank must hold against deposits
Supply-Side Economics: A macroeconomic theory arguing that economic growth can be most effectively created by lowering taxes and decreasing regulation
Structural Unemployment: Unemployment resulting from industrial reorganization rather than fluctuations in supply or demand
Stagflation: A situation in which the inflation rate is high, the economic growth rate slows, and unemployment remains steadily high
Sunk Cost: A cost that has already been incurred and cannot be recovered
Subsidy: A form of financial aid or support extended to an economic sector generally with the aim of promoting economic and social policy
Tariff: A tax imposed by a government on imported or exported goods
Trade Liberalization: The removal or reduction of restrictions or barriers on the free exchange of goods between nations
Terms of Trade: The ratio at which a country can trade its exports for imports from other countries
Tragedy of the Commons: A situation in which individuals acting independently and rationally according to their own self-interest deplete a shared limited resource
Tobin Tax: A tax on spot currency conversions designed to penalize short-term currency speculation
Unemployment Rate: The percentage of the total labor force that is unemployed but actively seeking employment
Underground Economy: Economic transactions that are not reported and therefore not taxed or monitored by the government
Utility Maximization: The concept that individuals and firms seek to get the greatest value possible from their resources
Underemployment: A condition in which people are employed at less than full-time or in jobs inadequate for their training or economic needs
Unit Elasticity: A situation where a change in price results in a proportional change in quantity demanded or supplied
Value Added Tax (VAT): A consumption tax placed on a product whenever value is added at each stage of the supply chain
Velocity of Money: The rate at which money circulates in the economy
Vertical Integration: A strategy where a company expands its business operations into different steps on the same production path
Volatility: A statistical measure of the dispersion of returns for a given security or market index
Venture Capital: Financing provided to startups and small businesses with high growth potential
Wage Stickiness: A situation where wages do not fall even in the face of high unemployment
World Trade Organization (WTO): An intergovernmental organization that regulates international trade
Wealth Effect: The change in spending that accompanies changes in perceived wealth
Working Capital: The difference between a company's current assets and current liabilities
Welfare Economics: The study of how the allocation of resources affects economic well-being
Wage-Price Spiral: A cycle in which rising wages cause higher prices and higher prices cause higher wages
X-Efficiency: The degree of efficiency maintained by firms under conditions of imperfect competition
Xenocurrency: Currency traded in markets outside of its domestic borders
Xenoeconomics: The study of foreign or external economic systems and influences
Xenophobia in Trade: The fear or dislike of foreign goods and foreign trade
X-Inefficiency: A situation where a firm is not producing at minimum cost due to lack of competitive pressure
Yield Curve: A graph showing the relationship between bond yields and maturities
Yield to Maturity (YTM): The total return anticipated on a bond if the bond is held until it matures
Yardstick Competition: A method of performance measurement in regulated industries
Yen Carry Trade: A strategy involving borrowing in low-interest rate yen to invest in higher yielding currencies
Youth Unemployment: The unemployment of young people, typically aged 15–24
Zero-Sum Game: A situation in which one participant's gain is exactly balanced by the losses of others
Zero Inflation Targeting: A monetary policy aiming for exactly zero inflation
Zoning Laws: Legal restrictions on land use specifying the areas in which residential, industrial, recreational or commercial activities may take place
Zero Coupon Bond: A bond that does not pay interest but is traded at a deep discount, rendering profit at maturity
