SCADA (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition): A high-level system architecture that combines computers, networked data communications, and graphical user interfaces for the real-time monitoring and control of large-scale industrial processes and infrastructure
Ferranti Effect: A phenomenon occurring in long, unloaded or lightly loaded transmission lines where the voltage at the receiving end of the line becomes significantly higher than the voltage at the sending end
Smart Grid: A modernized electrical grid that integrates bidirectional digital communication technology and automated systems to monitor and manage the transport of electricity from all generation sources to meet the varying electricity demands of end-users
Shannon-Hartley Theorem: A fundamental formula in communication theory that establishes the maximum rate (channel capacity) at which information can be transmitted over a communication channel of a specific bandwidth in the presence of noise
Root Locus: A graphical method used in control system design to examine how the roots of a system's characteristic equation (and thus its stability) change in the s-plane as a specific parameter, typically the open-loop gain, is varied
State-Space Representation: A mathematical model of a physical system as a set of input, output, and state variables related by first-order differential equations, allowing for the analysis of multi-input multi-output (MIMO) systems
Total Harmonic Distortion (THD): A measurement of the harmonic distortion present in a signal, defined as the ratio of the sum of the powers of all harmonic components to the power of the fundamental frequency, used to assess the quality of power systems and audio equipment
Eddy Currents: Loops of electrical current induced within a conductor by a changing magnetic field, which result in energy loss through heat (Joule heating) in components like transformer cores and electric motors
OSI Model (Open Systems Interconnection): A conceptual framework that standardizes the functions of a telecommunication or computing system into seven distinct layers, ranging from the Physical layer to the Application layer, to ensure interoperability between different systems
Common Mode Rejection Ratio (CMRR): A metric for differential amplifiers that quantifies the device's ability to reject input signals common to both input leads while amplifying the difference between them
Crosstalk: Any phenomenon where a signal transmitted on one circuit or channel of a transmission system creates an undesired effect in another circuit or channel, a critical factor in PCB layout and high-speed data communication
